The SDK's k230_gzip() (buildroot-overlay/board/canaan/k230-soc/post-image.sh)
tries -n8, then -n9, -n7, -n6, -n5, -n4. In gzip's getopt string
'ab:cdfhH?klLmMnNqrS:tvVZ123456789' the n option takes no argument, so
-nN parses as -n -N. Both tools were run at every level the SDK falls
through:

  for lvl in 4 5 6 7 8 9; do
    ./k230_priv_gzip -n$lvl -f -k u-boot.bin && mv u-boot.bin.gz vendor-n$lvl.gz
    gzip -n -$lvl -f -k u-boot.bin        && mv u-boot.bin.gz nixpkgs-n$lvl.gz
  done

Identical at every level. -n8, the level the SDK actually uses, gives
2051b07f1edbf8aebca5f512b1f72eba33b9c97934b4bb8c2ed0cfd1039e57d3 from
both, matching the figure docs/blob-inventory.md §A4 recorded on 2026-09-20.

This is the whole argument for replacing the vendor binary with pkgs.gzip
in nix/stage1.nix, and it is empirical: whatever Canaan changed in their
copy of gzip 1.6, it does not change the bytes. The vendor-specific step
is the sed on the next line of post-image.sh, which flips the gzip CM
byte from 0x08 to 0x09; nix/stage1.nix carries that sed.
